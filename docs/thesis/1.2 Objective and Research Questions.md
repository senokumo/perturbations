The goal is to explore and evaluate methods to generate synthetic live-audio datasets, through augmentation via data-driven perturbation.

It is studied, if and how much this approach is able to create data that resemble characteristics of real live-music-data.

The thesis aims to answer the following research questions:

1. How do Live-Performances of the same piece vary in their audio-features, how do correlations of these features look like?

2. What combinations of perturbations and optimizations create synthetic data of which features resemble these of real data?

3. How is the quality of the augmentation evaluated statistically and perceptually plausible?
